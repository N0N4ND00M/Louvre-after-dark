The scripts below are intentionally short and clear.

01. Mona Lisa
FILE: mona.mp3
Before you is the Mona Lisa. Leonardo da Vinci worked on this portrait over many years. Look closely at the face. There are almost no hard outlines. Instead, light and shadow fade gently into one another — a technique known as sfumato. It is one reason her expression can seem to change while you look at her.

02. Winged Victory of Samothrace
FILE: victory.mp3
Before you stands the Winged Victory of Samothrace. The sculpture represents Nike, the Greek goddess of victory. Notice the forward movement of the body and the deeply carved folds of fabric. The stone seems almost weightless — as though wind is still moving across it.

03. Venus de Milo
FILE: venus.mp3
This is the Venus de Milo. Her missing arms remain one of the sculpture's great mysteries. Notice how the weight shifts through the body. That gentle curve creates movement and balance, even though the figure itself is completely still.

04. Liberty Leading the People
FILE: liberty.mp3
Liberty Leading the People turns revolution into theatre. At the centre, Liberty is both a real woman and an allegorical figure. Delacroix surrounds her with people from different social groups, pushing forward through smoke, rubble and bodies. The diagonal movement makes the painting feel as if the crowd is entering our space.

05. The Coronation of Napoleon
FILE: coronation.mp3
This immense canvas records a carefully staged moment of imperial power. Napoleon stands at the centre while Joséphine kneels before him. David fills the cathedral with portraits, fabric, architecture and ceremony. The result looks documentary, yet every detail has been arranged to strengthen the image of authority.

06. The Raft of the Medusa
FILE: medusa.mp3
The Raft of the Medusa is built around a terrible contrast: despair below, hope above. Bodies form a rising pyramid that leads toward the figures waving at a tiny ship on the horizon. The sea, sky and diagonals keep the entire image unstable. Géricault turns a recent disaster into a monumental history painting.

07. The Wedding Feast at Cana
FILE: cana.mp3
Across from the Mona Lisa hangs one of the Louvre's largest paintings. Veronese turns the Wedding at Cana into a dazzling Venetian world. Musicians, servants, guests, animals and architecture compete for attention. Yet Christ remains remarkably still at the centre, creating calm inside the visual noise.

08. The Lacemaker
FILE: lacemaker.mp3
The Lacemaker is tiny compared with many Louvre masterpieces, but its focus is intense. Vermeer brings us unusually close to a young woman working with thread. The foreground dissolves into soft colour while the hands and face hold our attention. The painting turns concentration itself into the subject.

09. The Astronomer
FILE: astronomer.mp3
In The Astronomer, knowledge becomes a physical action. The scholar reaches toward a celestial globe while daylight enters from the left. Books, instruments and the map create a world of study, but Vermeer keeps the scene intimate. Science here is not spectacle. It is attention, curiosity and thought.

10. Code of Hammurabi
FILE: hammurabi.mp3
This dark stone stele is both an image and a text. At the top, Hammurabi stands before the god Shamash. Below them, hundreds of lines of cuneiform set out laws and judgments. The object combines political authority, religion and writing in a single monumental form.

11. The Seated Scribe
FILE: scribe.mp3
The Seated Scribe feels startlingly present. His body is relaxed, but the face is alert. The eyes were made with several materials, including rock crystal, producing an unusually vivid gaze. Instead of an idealised ruler, we meet a working official captured with individual detail.

12. Great Sphinx of Tanis
FILE: sphinx.mp3
The Great Sphinx of Tanis compresses royal power into a hybrid body: human intelligence joined to the strength of a lion. Its surface also carries traces of different rulers, reminding us that monuments could be reused and reinscribed across centuries of Egyptian history.

13. The Oath of the Horatii
FILE: horatii.mp3
This is The Oath of the Horatii, painted by Jacques-Louis David in 1784. Three brothers reach toward the swords held by their father. Their straight bodies and strong gestures create a feeling of discipline and duty. On the right, the women collapse into grief. David places public duty and private emotion in the same scene.

14. The Intervention of the Sabine Women
FILE: sabines.mp3
David painted The Intervention of the Sabine Women in 1799. At the centre, Hersilia throws herself between the opposing leaders. Her body forms a barrier against the weapons around her. Children and families appear between the soldiers. The painting changes a battle scene into an image about stopping violence.

15. Grande Odalisque
FILE: odalisque.mp3
Ingres painted Grande Odalisque in 1814. At first the figure appears calm and realistic. Look longer, and the anatomy begins to feel unusual. The back is deliberately elongated and the pose is almost impossible. Ingres preferred the elegance of the line over strict anatomical accuracy.

16. Psyche Revived by Cupid's Kiss
FILE: psyche.mp3
Canova created Psyche Revived by Cupid's Kiss between 1787 and 1793. Cupid bends over Psyche at the moment she returns to life. Their arms create a circular movement around the two faces. Canova makes cold marble appear soft, light and almost weightless.

17. Dying Slave
FILE: dyingslave.mp3
Michelangelo carved the Dying Slave between 1513 and 1515 for the tomb of Pope Julius the Second. The head tilts back and the body twists gently. The pose can look like sleep, surrender or death. Michelangelo uses the tension between relaxed and active muscles to keep the figure emotionally uncertain.

18. The Cheat with the Ace of Diamonds
FILE: cheat.mp3
This is The Cheat with the Ace of Diamonds by Georges de La Tour. The young player on the right does not realise what is happening. Across the table, looks and gestures connect the other figures. The cheat hides a card behind his back. The entire story is told without movement, only through hands, eyes and timing.
