LOUVRE AFTER DARK — STUDIO NARRATION ADDENDUM (WORKS 19–24)

19. Saint John the Baptist
Filename: assets/audio/saintjohn.mp3
Saint John the Baptist emerges almost entirely from darkness. Leonardo uses sfumato so the edges of the face, hair and shoulder seem to dissolve into shadow. The raised finger points upward, directing attention beyond the picture. The smile is quiet and ambiguous, giving the sacred subject a strangely intimate presence.

20. Portrait of Baldassare Castiglione
Filename: assets/audio/castiglione.mp3
Raphael gives Baldassare Castiglione extraordinary presence without dramatic action. The portrait uses a narrow range of greys, blacks and warm skin tones. His hands are calm, his gaze direct, and the soft modelling makes the sitter feel both dignified and approachable. The simplicity of the composition concentrates attention on character.

21. The Turkish Bath
Filename: assets/audio/turkishbath.mp3
The Turkish Bath is one of Ingres's final major works. Its circular format turns the scene into a private world crowded with repeated curves, backs, arms and fabrics. The figures are not observed from one real place. Instead, Ingres recombines poses and ideas from decades of earlier studies into an imagined interior.

22. The Valpinçon Bather
Filename: assets/audio/valpincon.mp3
The Valpinçon Bather turns away from us. Ingres avoids narrative and builds the picture from long, controlled contours. The back is simplified and elongated, while the turban, curtain and folded cloth create different textures around the body. The stillness makes looking itself the subject.

23. Human-Headed Winged Bull
Filename: assets/audio/lamassu.mp3
This monumental guardian once stood at an Assyrian palace entrance. It combines human intelligence, the strength of a bull and the power of wings. From the front it appears firmly planted. From the side, an extra leg helps the creature look as if it is striding forward. The sculpture was designed to impress and protect at the same time.

24. Frieze of Archers
Filename: assets/audio/archers.mp3
These archers once formed part of the palace decoration at Susa under Darius the First. Their repeated profile creates order and rhythm. Glazed bricks preserve strong blues, greens, yellows and browns. The figures are ceremonial rather than individual portraits, turning disciplined repetition into an image of imperial power.
